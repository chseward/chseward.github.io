Christopher H. Seward, PhD

Staff Genomics Scientist · Pacific Northwest Research Institute (PNRI), Seattle

I am a genomics scientist working in long-read genetics and epigenetics. Long-read genomes read a person's variants and their DNA methylation from the same molecules; I build methods that use both at once, and apply them to X-linked disease, methylation episignatures, and how stress and metabolism shape pregnancy outcomes. I trained in microbiology (BS, University of Rochester) and took my PhD in Cell and Developmental Biology at the University of Illinois with Lisa Stubbs, working on the genomics of social behaviour, stress and metabolism. PNRI made my position independent in 2025, and since July 2025 I have worked with Claudia Carvalho’s group, supported by her grants. Building on the epigenomics I did with Lisa, that work has given me deep experience in clinical human genomics: structural variants, long reads, DNA methylation and rare-disease family studies. I also continue my own projects, and with Lisa Stubbs, who left PNRI in 2025, I am co-PI of a proposed study of stress and pregnancy.

X-chromosome inactivation from long reads (XCITE)

With Claudia Carvalho’s group, since July 2025

Every cell in a woman's body silences one of her two X chromosomes. When most cells silence the same one ("skewed" X-inactivation), it can protect a woman who carries a harmful X-linked variant, or leave her affected. The clinical test for skew (HUMARA) reads a single site, fails in many women, and cannot say which X carries the variant.

I developed XCITE, which measures X-inactivation from the PacBio HiFi or Oxford Nanopore long reads a lab already has. It separates each woman's two X chromosomes by repeat length and nearby variants, compares methylation between them at promoter CpG islands across the chromosome, and reports:

  • the skew ratio, chromosome-wide and region by region, calibrated against the clinical assay;
  • which X carries a variant (structural variants, indels or SNVs), and whether that X is the active or the silenced one;
  • the parent of origin of the variant when relatives are sequenced.

We applied it to a cohort of families with MECP2 duplications and other Xq28 rearrangements to ask why many women who carry these rearrangements are unaffected while others are affected, and where the rearrangements arise. XCITE agrees with the clinical HUMARA assay (r = 0.97, n = 12), and in a cohort of more than 80 women, 12 of 16 carrier mothers carried the duplication on the inactive X (ASHG 2026).

Presented: ASHG 2026 (poster, Montréal) and the Long-Read Sequencing Symposium 2026 (talk, Seattle Children's). Manuscripts in preparation with Claudia Carvalho’s group and collaborators at Baylor College of Medicine; preprint and code release in preparation.

Methylation episignatures

With Claudia Carvalho’s group, since July 2025

Many neurodevelopmental disorders leave a recognisable genome-wide methylation pattern, an "episignature", now used clinically on methylation arrays. Long-read genomes measure methylation at nearly every CpG with no extra assay, so the same data could detect the variant and its episignature together.

  • With Claudia Carvalho’s group and the EpiSign team (using EpiSign’s array cohort), testing whether the MECP2 duplication syndrome episignature defined on clinical methylation arrays can be read from PacBio long-read methylation data (manuscript in preparation).
  • Searching for new long-read signatures with held-out testing, permutation nulls and stability checks.
  • Built the controls (age exclusion, within-batch nulls, cross-sex scoring) that a long-read episignature needs, because age and methylation-caller batch can mimic a signature.

Genomics of stress, metabolism and social behaviour

PhD and postdoctoral work with Lisa Stubbs; senior author on the Tcf7l2 studies

How does a social challenge change the brain? Across mice, sticklebacks and honey bees we measured how gene expression and chromatin respond over time to social challenge and stress, and found a shared toolkit of responses, with metabolic and neural programmes switching on together.

  • Transcriptional and epigenomic time courses of the brain's response to social challenge.
  • Cross-species comparison of neurogenomic responses (mouse, fish, bee).
  • An epigenomic shift in the amygdala that marks the onset of maternal behaviour.
  • Mouse models of neurodevelopmental genes (AUTS2, Galnt17) linking molecular, brain and behavioural pathology.

My PhD showed that the WNT effector Tcf7l2, the strongest common genetic risk factor for type 2 diabetes, sits at the nexus of metabolic regulation, the stress response of hypothalamic cells and social behaviour. Mouse models that lose its long isoform across the brain or only in the hypothalamus show disturbed circadian rhythms, susceptibility to diet-induced obesity, and abnormalities of the thalamus and hypothalamus (two manuscripts in preparation, as senior author).

Stress and gestational diabetes

Co-PI with Lisa Stubbs · proposed genomics study

Gestational diabetes (GDM) is common and rising, and children exposed to it carry a higher lifetime risk of metabolic disease. Psychosocial stress is one of the suspected drivers. This work grew out of the Galas Project, a PNRI pilot study of the genetics of maternal stress and pregnancy complications, in which I characterized candidate genes such as TCF7L2 in clinical and model-system data. Since Lisa Stubbs left PNRI in 2025 I have continued my earlier projects with her on my own time, with PNRI’s approval. With her I am co-PI of a proposed genomics study, grown out of the Galas pilot, in a large, diverse, prospective pregnancy cohort that studies how stress leads to GDM; if funded, I would be its genomics lead.

  • Long-read genomes that give each woman's variants, structural variants and methylation from one assay, including the ancestries that genotyping arrays and imputation panels serve worst.
  • Blood RNA sequencing early and late in pregnancy, adding early-pregnancy signals to a predictor tested on held-out women.
  • Power modelling to keep the design focused on a predictive test that can be validated, with the same held-out testing I use for long-read methylation signatures.

Approach

Clinical human genomics

Rare-disease family studies across more than 2,000 genomes (the Carvalho lab’s full cohort and GREGoR Consortium data): SV breakpoints, methylation and inheritance, checked against clinical assays (HUMARA, aCGH, EpiSign).

Long-read genomics

PacBio HiFi and ONT: phasing, structural variants, tandem repeats, 5mC methylation.

Pipelines that scale

Cohort-scale analysis of more than 2,000 genomes on Slurm, AWS and AnVIL (GATK, Parliament2, SV and methylation calling), and tested Python pipelines with versioned, re-runnable studies.

Statistics with honest nulls

Permutation and held-out testing, batch and age confounds named and controlled.

Functional genomics

RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, ATAC-seq, Hi-C and capture Hi-C, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics.

Optical mapping and SVs

De novo structural variant analysis combining long reads, short reads and Bionano optical maps.

Imaging

Super-resolution confocal, 3D light-sheet and two-photon microscopy of cleared tissue.
